# applied/ - runs against real workspaces, by the builder

Not blind runs. These were produced by the author, who wrote the doctrine and the answer keys.
`TESTING.md` labels them that way and never counts them as blind. The blind suite is `B1` to `B8`
in `../`.

Two of the three are not published, and this file says exactly what they were and why they are
not here.

---

## A3 - Mimir diagnosed against the competition conditions

**Published**, in `A3-mimir-vs-brief.md`, together with the withdrawal in `A3-OVERTURNED.md`.

It named one cause, listed three observations that would overturn it, and the owner met the third
within hours. It is kept because a finding that wrote down its own falsifier and then lost to it
is worth more than one that was quietly deleted.

**One redaction.** The supplied evidence for that run included an excerpt from the owner's private
competition playbook. That document is not published, so link 7 of the finding quotes a source a
reader cannot check. The finding is preserved unedited and this is the disclosure; do not treat
that link as grounded.

## A1 and A2 - two failures in a private workspace

**Not published.** They diagnosed a working intake operator that runs against a company mailbox.
The workspace is private, its own entry file forbids publication, and the findings quote its
internal doctrine at length - the writing rules, the routing rules, the conflict rules. That is
the owner's employer's material and it does not belong in an MIT repository.

What is publishable is what the runs *demonstrated*, and none of it needs the quotes:

**A1 - the drafts came out generic.** The drafting step's declared inputs named the templates and
the house tone and never the project's own state, so every substantive slot in the template had
no source and fell through to a placeholder. The tone rules were the obvious suspect and the
wrong one: they are exclusively subtractive, and removing them yields a longer generic draft,
because length is not substance.

This run returned `OUT-OF-TAXONOMY`. The nearest three families each failed on a stated ground,
and the gap it found is now **family 20**, "the contract omits what the step needs". That is the
escape hatch doing the job it was built for, on a real folder, before anyone went looking for a
missing family. See `DEFECTS.md` D8.

**A2 - a draft was often not produced where one was expected.** The condition under which the
tool drafts a reply had three homes with three different scopes, and the workspace's own conflict
rule elevated the narrowest of them. Two ordering rules in the same file disagreed about which of
the other two won. In-taxonomy: the same fact with more than one authoritative home (family 11).

Both findings passed all gates against their evidence at the time they were written. Both later
failed the `PLAIN` gate, which did not exist yet - see `DEFECTS.md` C14.

## Why summaries rather than redacted findings

A receipt that has been edited is not a receipt. Replacing the quotes with neutral text would
have produced two documents that look verbatim, read as evidence, and are neither - which is
precisely the failure this folder exists to catch in other people's work.

So the findings are withheld whole, and what remains is a summary that is honest about being one.
The unedited originals are held by the owner, outside this repository.
